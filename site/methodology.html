Methodology

A bout is one public caller against THE MARKET. The entry, the target, and the deadline come from the caller's own post. Those three lines are the fight.

The call size

The call size is the percent move from the entry to the target. If a caller gives a range, only the lower number counts. A call from $100 to $120–$140 is a 20% call.

A win, and a KO

Hitting the call on the penny is a win. One penny past it is a knockout. A long from $100 to $120 wins at $120.00 and is a KO at $120.01.

THE MARKET is the mirror. On that same call, $80.00 is a win for THE MARKET, and $79.99 is a KO. The level is the call distance taken the other way from the entry. The KO is one penny past that level.

The against-distance is the smaller of the call distance and 50% of the entry, rounded down to the cent. The KO is one more penny past that. A call for a 100% move is not answered 100% the other way.

There is no stop on the card. THE MARKET's KO is what ends a bout the hard way. A stop a caller named is not used for now. A side condition in the post, such as "after hitting my $130 target," is ignored. Only the main target is scored.

A touch

A level is hit when price trades there, or when the market gaps open at or past it. Pre-market, after-hours, and a gap open count, along with the regular session. It does not have to be the close.

Every tick counts. A level is hit the moment any trade prints at it, even a single tick, at any hour. Pre-market, after-hours, overnight, and 24-hour markets all count. A thin market counts the same as a busy one. The only exception is a trade the exchange cancels as an error. Those don't count, and the card says so.

Only trades after the post minute count. The candle that sets the entry is not a touch. If the target is already reached at post time, the call is VOID.

The first touch of either side's level ends the bout. A later move cannot change the result. If both sides are touched in the same minute, the tie is broken with finer tick or second data. If that finer data still cannot tell which side was first, the bout is a DRAW.

How it ends

  • Win. Price hits the target on the penny, and does not go one penny past it.
  • Win by KO. Price trades one penny past the target.
  • Win by decision. No level is touched, and the deadline-day regular-session close is at or past the entry in the caller's direction. A close exactly at the entry goes to the caller.
  • Loss. Price hits THE MARKET's level on the penny, and does not go one penny past it.
  • Loss by KO. Price trades one penny past THE MARKET's level.
  • Loss by decision. No level is touched, and the deadline close is on THE MARKET's side of the entry.
  • Void. The target is already reached at post time. The bout is not scored.
  • Draw. Both sides are touched in the same minute, and finer data cannot tell which side was first.

The clock

Entry is the close of the 1-minute candle that contains the post. If that minute has no trade, including extended hours, entry is the close of the last 1-minute candle traded before it. The post time comes from the post ID: shift the ID right by 22 bits and add 1288834974657. That sum is the UTC time in milliseconds. Convert it to Eastern Time and use that minute.

The price source is Schwab, then Yahoo, then another named source. The card names the source that was used. Prices are never adjusted for dividends or splits. The percent on the card is rounded half-up to one decimal.

If no level is touched, the deadline-day regular-session close decides the bout.

Edge cases

Edge cases are handled when they come up. When a real bout hits a situation these rules don't cover:

  1. It is scored by the closest existing rule and marked under review on the card.
  2. The editor decides the new rule.
  3. The new rule is written on this page with a dated note.
  4. It applies to that bout and every later bout. Past locked results are not rescored unless the editor says so, and any change to a posted result goes on the corrections log (opens in a new tab).

Post proof

Each bout keeps the post ID, the UTC time from that ID, and the same time in Eastern Time. An archived copy is saved through archive.today or the Wayback Machine. A timestamped screenshot of the post is kept, and its SHA-256 hash is stored in the repo.

An edited post is scored from the original version, and the card notes the edit. A deleted post is scored from the archive and labeled "deleted by caller".

How calls are picked

Every qualifying call from a tracked caller in a set window goes on the card. A call is never cherry-picked by outcome. The wording stays neutral. The card scores only what the post said.

Corrections

A caller can ask for a correction for seven days after the result is posted. The request names the bout, the line, and the post. No personal address is published. Accepted corrections are written on the public corrections log (opens in a new tab), newest first. An edit or deletion at the source does not rewrite the locked call.
