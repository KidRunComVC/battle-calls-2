Methodology

Battle Calls locks a call before it is judged. The market is the referee. The horizon is chosen before the result, and it is not moved afterward.

  1. Round 1: Silver
  2. Round 2: Bitcoin
  3. Round 3: S&P 500

Lock the call

Each call is archived before judging: the URL when one exists, the post ID, the time in UTC and in ET, and the wording copied exactly. If the post is edited later, the locked wording is what gets judged. A deleted post is noted on the card and is not republished. The note says it was deleted, and it is not used for scoring in place of the repost.

Entry price

Posted during regular hours: the open of the first 1-minute bar after the post. Posted outside regular hours: the next regular-session open. For a venue that trades through the weekend, the card names the bar it used. Each person is scored from that person's own entry.

Price source and timestamp

Every bout names its price source and the timestamp of the print. A locked historical print is labeled as such. It is not a live quote. For crypto, one venue is named before judging. The default reference is the official close. A call that names a target or a stop is a touch call, judged on the intraday high or low, and the first to touch wins, unless the bout says it uses daily settles or daily closes only.

Horizons

Today means that day's close. This week means Friday's close. When no horizon is stated, the horizon is five trading days, and that choice is announced before judging. The horizon is never picked after the fact. When Battle Calls sets the horizon, the card says so.

Call types

A call is a direction, a target, or a relative comparison. A clean opposite is the same asset, an overlapping horizon, and opposite directions. A hedged or untimed call cannot be an official bout. It may run as a labeled EXHIBITION, with the rules set in advance. No contest is the result when a bout cannot be judged, for example when the data is unavailable or a call is withdrawn before the first bell.

When a bout defines a stop-hit that ends it before the bell, that finish is a TKO, not a KO. The card lists the checks in order, before any of them can hit. KO, TKO (stop hit), and decision are separate finishes.

How a result is named

Results are reported in percent and in points, to two decimals. A KO is the level named on the card. A decision is the bigger favorable move from each entry when no KO lands, using the rule that bout locked. A draw is a move smaller than the larger of 0.25% and half the 20-day average daily move, unless the bout has already published a tighter or wider band. The band on the card is the band that is used. If both targets hit, the first to touch wins, unless the bout is settle-only or close-only.

The scorecard is updated on a weekly check-in until the final bell. A lead on a check-in is not a winner.

Tale of the tape

The tape shows the corner, the name, the handle as text when there is a public handle, a profile link when there is one, the exact call, the link to the original post or broadcast, the time in ET, the price at the first bell, the KO line, the final bell, and a site-only record that starts at 0-0-0.

Account age and follower counts are shown only when a primary-source figure was archived with a timestamp. This launch card does not show them, because those figures were not archived that way. They are not estimated. A credential is shown only when it is verifiable at a primary source: the CMT directory, the CFA designation, BrokerCheck, or IAPD. Claimed returns, hit rates, and AUM are not shown.

A television call with no post is labeled as a TV call. The card quotes only the wording it has, and it says when the spoken wording is still pending a tape check. No profile link is invented.

Corrections

Mistakes by this site are listed on the corrections log, newest first. A correction names the bout, what changed, and why. An edit or deletion at the source does not rewrite the locked call.
